# Vendor Evaluation

## Use

Use when the user is choosing a tool, service or supplier, comparing two or more, or deciding whether to renew one.

The contract's clauses belong to `legal/contract-review`. Comparing your own product with rivals belongs to `marketing/competitor-analysis`. Establishing a current fact about a vendor from public sources belongs to `research/research-brief`.

## Domain inputs and blockers

- What the vendor is for and what would happen without it.
- The quote, plan or contract terms as supplied.
- For a renewal: what is paid now and how the service has actually performed, since a price increase cannot be judged without the current spend.
- How long the user expects to depend on it.

Prices, plan limits and features change; take them from the quote or the vendor's page opened in this session, with the date, and mark anything else as unverified.

## Cost

Compare on total cost of ownership, not the licence line:

- subscription or licence, including the tier the real usage will land in and the price after any introductory period;
- implementation and migration;
- training and the time to become productive;
- ongoing support and maintenance;
- exit: getting the data out, the notice period, any termination charge.

Show the total for the first year and for three years, since the licence line alone hides the cost of getting in and out.

## Risk

| Risk | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| financial stability | will the vendor exist and keep investing in the product |
| security and compliance | whether its security and compliance posture fits the data being handed over |
| concentration | how much of the business would stop if this one vendor failed |
| lock-in | proprietary formats, export limits, auto-renewal terms, switching effort |
| continuity | uptime commitments, support response, disaster recovery |

Rate each for likelihood and impact and state the mitigation. A risk with no mitigation is weighed in the decision, not listed and forgotten.

## Performance, for a renewal

Whether service levels were met, how fast support responded, reliability, whether promised features arrived, and how satisfied the people using it are.

## Comparison

Put the options side by side on the same rows: price, the features that matter for the stated purpose, integrations, security, support, contract terms, and evidence from reference customers. Features that will not be used are left out; they only make the longer list look better.

## Output

1. **Recommendation** in two or three sentences: proceed, negotiate, or pass.
2. **Cost table** with first-year and three-year totals.
3. **Risk table** with likelihood, impact and mitigation.
4. **Strengths and concerns.**
5. **Negotiation points**: what to ask for and what leverage exists, such as a competing quote, contract length or volume.

## Constraints

- Do not fill a missing price or limit with a remembered one.
- Do not recommend on features alone when the exit cost is unknown; say that it is unknown.
- The legal meaning of a contract term is a lawyer's call.
