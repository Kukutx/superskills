# Performance Review

## Use

Use when someone's work over a period has to be put into words: the user's own self-assessment, a review of a report, feedback that needs to be made specific, or preparation for a calibration meeting.

Assessing candidates belongs to `people/hiring`. Reflecting on one's own feelings about work belongs to `personal/psychology-reflection`.

## Domain inputs and blockers

A review is only as good as its facts. Needed: the period, the role and level, the goals that were set, and what the person actually did with dates, numbers or named outcomes. With no facts supplied, deliver the structure and the questions that would fill it, not a plausible review of an imagined person.

Which of the three modes applies is usually clear from who is writing; ask only when it is not.

## Writing rules

1. **Specific over general.** "You cut deployment time by 40% by rebuilding the release script" instead of "great job on releases".
2. **Behaviour, not character.** Describe what the person did and its effect, not what they are. "Missed three of five agreed deadlines in the second quarter" can be acted on; "unreliable" cannot.
3. **Both sides, and no surprises.** Strengths and development areas each carry an example. Anything critical in a written review should already have been said in person; when it has not, flag that to the writer.
4. **Development that can be done.** "Present the roadmap at the next all-hands" instead of "improve communication".
5. **Impact is tied to the role's level.** The same result can exceed expectations at one level and meet them at the next.

## Self-assessment

Three to five accomplishments, each as situation, own contribution, and impact with a number or named outcome. Then goals reviewed as met, exceeded or missed with the evidence; areas of growth; obstacles; goals for the next period that are specific and measurable; and feedback for the manager. Write "I" for what was personally done and credit others for the rest.

## Manager review

An overall rating on the organization's own scale; a two to three sentence summary; strengths with examples; development areas with examples; goal attainment; a development plan (skill, where it stands, target, action); and the pay or promotion recommendation with its reasoning, kept separate from the feedback text the employee reads if the organization separates them.

## Calibration prep

A team table (name, role, level, tenure, proposed rating); the distribution compared with the organization's guidance, if it has one; the cases that need discussion (borderline, first review at a new level, recent change of role); promotion candidates with evidence of working at the next level; proposed pay actions; and context the room needs, such as a reorganization or a cancelled project.

A distribution target is the organization's policy; use the one the user gives and do not assume one.

## Answering a policy question from a handbook

Answer in a sentence or two, quote the passage with its section, name the exceptions, and say who decides edge cases. When the supplied text does not cover the question, say so; do not reconstruct the policy from what is usual elsewhere.

## Output

The document for the mode requested, ready to paste into the organization's form. When rewriting vague feedback, give the replacement text and the missing fact needed for each line that could not be made specific.

## Constraints

- Do not invent accomplishments, incidents, metrics or quotations.
- Nothing about health, family, age, leave taken or any protected characteristic belongs in a review.
- A review that supports a pay cut, demotion or dismissal is checked with HR or an employment lawyer before it is delivered.
- Pay figures stay inside the conversation's output and are not repeated in text meant for wider sharing.
