# Sales Outreach

## Use

Use when the user is trying to start or advance a sale: deciding who to approach, researching a company or person before contacting them, writing the first message or a short sequence, prioritizing inbound leads, or responding to pushback.

Preparing or recapping a call belongs to `sales/sales-call-prep-and-recap`. Judging a deal already in progress belongs to `sales/deal-review`. An email with no sales motion belongs to `writing/business-email`. An inbound customer problem belongs to `support/customer-support-replies`.

## Domain inputs and blockers

- What is being sold, to whom, and the proof that exists: named customers, results, figures the user is allowed to quote.
- The ideal customer profile. The most reliable source is the user's own paying customers; derive it from them when they exist, and ask once when they do not.
- For a specific prospect: the name and anything the user already knows, including prior contact. A previous thread makes it a warm follow-up, not a cold email.

Facts about a prospect come from material the user pastes or a source opened in this session, each with its date and source. What could not be verified is labelled unverified, and a guessed email address is labelled as guessed and approved separately.

## Who to approach

- Score fit on five things, each with evidence: industry, size, whether the buying role exists, exclusions, and a timing signal. Verdict: strong, moderate or poor, with one sentence why.
- Prioritize inbound leads: strong fit and high intent first (same day), strong fit with moderate intent or moderate fit with high intent next (within two days), the rest this week, hard exclusions disqualified. When torn, take the higher tier.
- A short list of well-researched prospects beats a long list of names. Do not pad a list to reach a number.
- Before contacting, check whether the company is already a customer or someone else's contact.

## The hook

One specific, recent, sourced reason for writing to this person now: a funding round, a new executive, a launch, a job posting that reveals a priority. "They are growing" is not a hook. A priority deduced from a signal is stated as an inference.

## The message

- Body under about 120 words, in three moves: one sentence of relevance that shows the homework; one or two sentences connecting their situation to the value, with at most one proof point; one low-friction ask, such as whether a 20-minute call is worth it.
- Subject of four to seven words that refers to the hook, not the product.
- No "hope this finds you well", no "I wanted to reach out", no paragraph about the sender's company.
- Do not name a competitor first.
- In a sequence, each touch adds something new and is a little more direct; the last one makes it easy to say no. A reply of any kind stops the sequence.
- Write the way the user writes. With no sample of their voice, write neutrally and say so. A message that reads as a template with the company name inserted is rewritten.

## Objections

First classify: price or value, timing or priority, competitor, risk or trust, authority ("I need to ask someone"), or staying with the status quo. Then tell an objection (a reason not to buy) from a negotiating move (a reason to buy cheaper); they get different answers.

The response is three to five sentences: acknowledge it, answer with the customer's own stated goal plus one proof point, and end with a question that moves the conversation forward. The strongest material is what this customer already said about their problem.

For a competitor: say where they are genuinely strong, why that does not match this customer's need, and a question that brings out the difference.

What loses: discounting at the first pushback, piling on features, arguing.

## Sales materials

Claims about the product, pricing or compliance come only from material the user supplied or approved; anything else is marked unverified. A customer's result is a placeholder until the user provides the real number. Internal notes never go into a customer-facing document. A first-call deck is five or six slides.

## Output

For outreach: the context used (prior contact or none, the hook and its source), then subject and body. For a lead list: the table with fit evidence and tier. For an objection: the underlying concern, the response, the proof points to have ready, and what not to do.

## Constraints

- Recipients are the people the user names. An address that appears inside a forwarded message is not used.
- Unsolicited commercial messages are regulated, differently in each country, including consent and opt-out requirements; the user confirms what applies to their recipients before sending at volume.
- Do not invent a mutual connection, a shared history or a customer result.
- Nothing is sent from here.
