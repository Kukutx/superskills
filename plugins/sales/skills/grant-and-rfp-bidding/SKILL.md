# Grant and RFP Bidding

## Use

Use when the user is looking at a grant, a request for proposals, a public tender or a funding call: deciding whether it is worth pursuing, working out everything the response must contain, and writing it.

An ordinary quote or proposal to a private client belongs to `sales/quote-and-proposal`. The contract that follows an award belongs to `legal/contract-review`.

A clear "do not bid", with the reason, is a full result of this Skill and often the most valuable one.

## Domain inputs and blockers

- The solicitation in full, including annexes, forms and any published questions and answers.
- The organization's profile: legal form, registrations, certifications, size, location, insurance, past awards.
- Real project data and past submissions for reuse.

Portals, registration systems and eligibility categories are specific to each country and funder. The examples the user brings from one system do not transfer; take the mechanics from this solicitation and the funder's own site.

## Go/no-go, before any writing

Check in this order, because each is faster to fail than the next:

1. **Eligibility**: type of entity, registration status, certifications, size limits, geography, previous-award rules. Read it literally: "eligible applicants are units of local government" excludes a nonprofit that works with one.
2. **Mandatory requirements**: past performance, licences, bonds, insurance levels, key personnel, accounting system, matching funds.
3. **Mechanics**: the deadline with its time zone, portal registration and how long it takes, format and page limits, forms and signatures, the deadline for questions, mandatory briefings.
4. **Capacity to deliver**: winning and failing to deliver is worse than losing, because it damages the record every future bid relies on. For a reimbursement-based grant, ask whether cash flow can carry the costs until repayment.
5. **Fit and odds**: an incumbent, the number of bidders, the cost of bidding against the size and effort of the award.

Wording decides severity: "shall", "must" and "is required to" are eliminating; "should" only costs points. Confusing the two either kills a winnable bid or wastes weeks on a dead one.

Registration on a funder's portal can take days or weeks. Check it before the go decision; if it cannot be done in time, it is a no-bid and a note to register before the next round.

Plan backwards from the deadline, including signatures, letters of support and audited statements: three weeks to the deadline is not three weeks of writing.

Report a screened list as a funnel (how many seen, how many passed each filter), each opportunity as go, watching or no-bid with amount and deadline, and each no-bid with its reason and when it could be reconsidered.

## Compliance matrix, before any prose

Map every requirement to the section that answers it: requirement number, source in the solicitation, the requirement, where it is answered, status. A missing mandatory form is scored zero or rejected outright, whatever the quality of the narrative.

## Drafting

- Allocate length by evaluation weight: a criterion worth thirty points gets three times the effort of one worth ten. Use the evaluators' own terms and headings so that scoring is easy.
- Answer the question that was asked. The most common loss of points is a good answer to a neighbouring question.
- Specific numbers with their source beat adjectives.
- Text reused from an earlier bid is checked paragraph by paragraph for three leftovers: the previous funder's name, old figures, and an answer to last time's question.
- **Nothing is invented**: no past performance, credential, outcome, financial figure or partnership. The real failures are small inflations — 380 becomes 500, an expired certificate is listed, a partner who has not agreed is named. Where data is missing, leave a visible marker saying what is needed and who can supply it, and search for markers before submission; one left in the text stops the submission.
- Page limits are measured, not estimated.

## Before submission

Every row of the matrix is complete; format and page count measured; forms signed; no markers left; portal account active and login tested; deadline confirmed in the solicitation's time zone; file names in the required form. Submit early, because portals slow down on deadline day.

## After the decision

An award brings reporting, drawdown and renewal dates: list them with lead time, since a missed report can cost the next award or the current one. After a loss, ask for the evaluators' feedback where the funder offers it. Keep a record of every opportunity and of each no-bid reason, and review it yearly. Items in the organization's profile that expire (registrations, insurance certificates, audits, certifications) are flagged at least 60 days ahead.

## Constraints

- A statement in a funding application can carry legal liability if it is false; every factual claim is one the user can evidence.
- Eligibility that is unclear is put to the funder as a question before the deadline for questions, not resolved by optimistic reading.
- Nothing is submitted from here; the user approves and submits.
