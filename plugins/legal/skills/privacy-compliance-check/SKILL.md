# Privacy Compliance Check

## Use

Use when the question is whether something involving personal data may be done, what must be in place first, or how to answer someone exercising their data rights.

Commercial terms of an agreement belong to `legal/contract-review`. This Skill gives an orientation for a non-lawyer; a regulator's letter, a breach or anything contested goes to a privacy lawyer, and the output says so.

## Domain inputs and blockers

Three facts decide almost everything: what is being done, which personal data it touches (and whether any is sensitive or about children), and where the people are. Ask for whichever of the three is missing; without the location, state which regimes were assumed.

Deadlines and thresholds change and differ by country. The figures below were checked against the law's text for GDPR and against the California Attorney General's guidance for CCPA; anything else, and anything decisive, is verified against the current official source before it is relied on.

## Checking an activity

Give one of three verdicts: **proceed**, **proceed with conditions** (list them), or **needs further review** (say by whom).

Under GDPR, look for:

- a lawful basis recorded for each purpose, and consent that is specific and withdrawable where consent is the basis;
- a data protection impact assessment before high-risk processing;
- a transfer mechanism for data leaving the EEA;
- a data processing agreement with every processor;
- breach readiness: the supervisory authority is notified without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware; a processor notifies its controller without undue delay.

Under CCPA/CPRA, look for: notice at collection, the rights to know, delete, correct, opt out of sale or sharing, and limit the use of sensitive personal information, and a working way to exercise them.

Other regimes (Brazil's LGPD, China's PIPL, Singapore's PDPA and others) set their own deadlines and transfer rules; name the regime and check its current text instead of applying GDPR numbers.

## A request from an individual

1. Identify the request: access, deletion, correction, portability, objection or opt-out.
2. Identify the regime from where the person is.
3. Verify identity in proportion to the sensitivity of the data; do not ask for more than is needed.
4. Log the date received. GDPR: respond within one month, extendable by two further months for complex or numerous requests, with the person told of the extension within the first month. CCPA: respond within 45 calendar days, extendable once by 45 more with notice.
5. Gather the data, apply any exemption and record which one. Data under a legal hold is not deleted; legally required records are kept; other people's data is not disclosed.
6. Reply. A refusal cites its specific ground and tells the person how to complain.

Stop and hand over to a lawyer, providing only a draft marked for counsel review, when the request comes from or concerns a minor, a regulator or a court, a current or former employee in a dispute, data under legal hold, special-category data, or when its scope looks like evidence gathering.

## Reviewing a data processing agreement

It should name the subject, duration, nature and purpose of the processing and the data categories; bind the processor to documented instructions and confidentiality; require notice of and a right to object to subprocessors; set a breach notice period short enough for the controller to meet its own 72 hours (24 to 48 hours is the usual ask); give audit rights; and require deletion or return at the end. For transfers out of the EEA, check that the current standard contractual clauses and the right module are used.

## Output

The verdict, the conditions or gaps with what would close each one, the deadline that applies and the date it falls on, and what must go to a lawyer. For a request, the reply draft with its deadline.

## Constraints

- Do not state that something is compliant; state what was checked and what was found.
- Do not apply one country's deadline to another.
- Do not draft a reply to a subpoena, a regulator or a litigation hold as if it were routine.
- A privacy policy describes what the product really does; do not write practices into it that are not in place.
