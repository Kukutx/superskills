# Page structure and refreshing old content

Read when building a whole page section by section, or when deciding whether an old page is refreshed or rewritten.

## Tests for a headline or a benefit line

- **"Now you can…"** Put those words in front of the line. If the result is both attractive and true, keep it. A description ("Powerful analytics platform") and an unfalsifiable promise ("Streamline your workflow") fail; a specific true statement ("See which companies visit your site") passes. It is a filter, not a formula: draft first, then test each line.
- **Clear beats clever.** If the reader has to stop and work out what is meant, the line has lost.
- **The same benefit reads differently by segment.** "Ship in a weekend" tells a startup "fast" and an enterprise "immature"; "enterprise-grade security" tells the enterprise "safe" and the startup "bloated"; "trusted by the Fortune 500" tells a small team "not for me". When one page must serve two kinds of reader, do not average them into one vague message: split the traffic by page or by role, and write each version.

## The top of the page

Three beats, and a reader stalls when one is missing: the present discomfort in the reader's own words; the better state; the path to it, where the product is the bridge and the action is the step onto it. They usually sit in the line above the headline or the subhead, the headline or subhead, and the action with its proof.

## Sections

A page that only lists features is a catalogue, not an argument.

| Section | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | describes the reader's problem better than they would, and hints at the cost of leaving it |
| Benefits | three to five, not ten; each a result as its heading, one or two sentences on how, optional proof |
| How it works | three or four numbered steps, each starting with a plain verb and saying what the reader gets |
| Social proof | logos that are recognized rather than numerous; quotations with a specific result, the before and after, and a role and company. "Great product!" is not proof |
| FAQ | the five to ten most common objections |
| Final action | restates the value, repeats the primary action, adds the risk reducer that is true (guarantee, trial, cancel any time) |

By situation:

- **Page behind an ad**: compact. Hero, proof, three benefits, a quotation, three steps, the action with its guarantee.
- **Page for large organizations**: add security and compliance, integrations, use cases by role, and the return on investment.
- **Launch page**: add a demonstration, a before and after, the launch price or early offer, and urgency only when it is real.

## Editing passes beyond the five in the entry

Read the whole piece once without changing anything. Then, as well as the entry's passes:

- **Clarity**: each sentence is understood at once; no unexplained jargon; every pronoun has an obvious referent; no sentence trying to do several jobs.
- **Voice and tone**: one level of formality, no sudden mood change, reads well aloud.
- **Feeling**: the copy should make the reader feel something as well as inform; the pain is real, the hope is achievable, and the emotion serves the message honestly.

After all changes, confirm the core message is still there.

## Refreshing an old page

Refresh when traffic is falling, the figures are more than a year old, the product or price has changed, competitors have updated the same topic, or the page should be quotable by AI search.

Six passes: currency (dates, figures, examples, retired features); accuracy (links, prices, feature descriptions); voice (old pages often carry the company's early tone); search (has the intent behind the query changed; show "Last updated" clearly); proof (newer quotations and numbers); structure (comparison tables, an FAQ, other scannable forms).

| Situation | Do |
| --- | --- |
| core message right, details stale | refresh |
| brand voice has changed a lot | refresh and rewrite the tone |
| the angle or audience changed, or the structure no longer matches the search intent | rewrite |
| only figures and links are stale | light refresh |

Review rhythm, as a working default with no study behind it: pricing and product pages each quarter or on change; high-traffic posts every six months; comparison and alternative pages every three to six months; evergreen guides yearly; low-traffic pages only when data shows an opening.
