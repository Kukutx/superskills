# Short video in detail

Read when writing a script beyond the basic structures in the entry, choosing a creator-led format, deciding on sound, or working out why videos underperform. Producing the footage belongs to `creative/promo-video`.

## More structures

- **Story arc**, 45–60 seconds: 0–3 s tease the result; 3–15 s the background and what was at stake; 15–45 s what happened; 45–55 s the result; 55–60 s the lesson or the action. For personal stories and case studies.
- **Point-of-view or sketch**, 15–30 seconds: 0–3 s text sets the scene; 3–25 s the situation is acted out; 25–30 s the punchline or turn. For recognition and humour.

Pacing by kind of footage: a slide-style video holds each slide two to four seconds with the key point as text; cutaway footage runs one to three seconds a shot, cut on the beat of the narration, mixing wide, medium and close; a screen recording zooms to the area that matters, highlights the cursor and moves smoothly; a talking shot needs steady light and eyes on the lens.

The script sheet: platform, length, format; the hook (seen, said, text); the body with timestamps; the action (spoken, text, gesture); production notes (music, extra footage, graphics).

## Founder and creator formats

A founder's own content is often a brand's first strong performer, because explaining why the product exists reaches people with the same problem.

- **The journey**: problem, background, attempts, failure, the realization, the breakthrough, what is still open. The weight is on how the events are reinterpreted, not on the events. One good story a week can do more than a post every day.
- **The numbers**: a cost breakdown or a fixed-budget challenge. "Surprisingly cheap" beats expensive; luxury is not a hook.
- **The rare sight**: something the audience has not seen and the user has access to, such as a workshop, a machine or a process. Money and luxury are not it.
- **The guide with a professional's eye**: "what I'd avoid as an interior designer". A learner's view also works, said honestly as that.

A talking-head piece opens with a strong view or a story, not a product claim; the product arrives as the turn in the story, not as a feature list. Choosing the wrong person fails more often than choosing the wrong script.

A reaction followed by a demonstration is short: the text over the reaction is the reactor's inner thought, not a product claim, and it cuts straight to the demonstration. A split-screen tutorial shows input on one side and result on the other, steps as text on the dividing line, music and no narration.

An expert on camera is a real expert making claims that are true and provable; health, medical and financial claims go through legal review; credentials are never faked and lines are never invented for the expert. Someone else's video used in a paid ad needs that creator's permission.

## Sound

- An organic post usually leaves music out of the file and adds a sound inside the app, where it can be discovered through that sound. A paid ad, or anywhere a native sound cannot be attached, has the music in the file.
- A trending sound is used only when it fits the content and is still rising. Teaching, stories, demonstrations and anything building a recognizable brand voice use original sound.
- Music sits under the narration, and a short fade at the end avoids an abrupt cut. A format with no narration where the sound is the point is the exception.
- Copyrighted music outside the platform's own library is avoided.

## When videos underperform

Starting points, to be checked against the account's own figures:

| Sign | Look at |
| --- | --- |
| several videos in a row that almost nobody finishes | the hook |
| many views, few follows | the action, and the fit between content and audience |
| many saves, few shares | useful, but nothing in it to pass on |
| many comments, mostly negative | whether the controversy is wanted, or the tone |

What each measure says: share of the video watched (does it hold throughout), completion (did the content keep the hook's promise), saves (worth returning to), shares (worth passing on), comments (started a conversation), follows (people want more). Test one variable at a time: hook, length, format, posting time or action.
