# Sector and Stock Screening

## Use

Use when the subject is a group of companies: understanding how an industry works and who wins in it, finding candidates that fit a style, or following a theme through its value chain.

Work on one company belongs to `finance/stock-research`; its value to `finance/company-valuation`. An industry overview with no investment angle, such as sizing a market for a product, belongs to `research/research-brief`.

A screen produces candidates, not conclusions. Every name that comes out of it still needs its own research, and nothing here is a recommendation.

## Domain inputs and blockers

- For a sector view: how widely the industry is drawn, how deep to go, whether the view is neutral or argues a thesis, and whether private companies count.
- For a screen: direction (long, short or both), size, region, style or theme.
- **Data.** A real quantitative screen needs a market database that a chat does not have. Give the criteria in a form the user can enter into their own screener, and review the list they paste back. Market figures quoted in the output carry a source and date; a market size without its source and method is not used.

## Sector overview

1. **Size and growth**: the market size with its source, historical growth, the forecast with its assumptions, and the split by product, region and customer type. Separate the headline market from the part that can realistically be reached, and flag figures that may be stale.
2. **Structure**: concentration (share of the top five), where in the value chain the profit accumulates, the business models in use, and the barriers to entry.
3. **Forces**: three to five long-term tailwinds, the headwinds, technology and regulatory change, consolidation.
4. **Competitors**: a table of the leading five to ten with revenue, growth, margin, share and what differentiates each; who is gaining or losing share and why; how they compete (price, product, service, distribution).
5. **Valuation context**: the range of multiples now and historically, what earns a premium or discount, recent deal multiples.
6. **The debate**: close with the bull and bear arguments and the events that could change the story.

## Screens

The thresholds are common starting filters, not tested rules, and they do not fit every industry (financials and unprofitable growth companies in particular). Adjust them and say what was used.

| Style | Typical filters |
| --- | --- |
| value | earnings multiple below the industry median; enterprise multiple below its own history; free cash flow yield above 5%; price to book below 1.5; recent insider buying |
| growth | revenue growth above 15%; profit growth above 20%; growth accelerating; margins expanding; return on invested capital above 15% |
| quality | five or more years of rising revenue; stable or expanding margins; return on equity above 15%; low debt; high conversion of profit into cash; meaningful insider ownership |
| red flags (short or avoid) | slowing revenue; shrinking margins; receivables or inventory growing faster than sales; insider selling; a premium valuation with no reason; a change of auditor or a restatement |
| special situations | lock-up expiry after a listing; a spin-off; recovery after restructuring; an activist shareholder; new management at an underperformer |

The best ideas tend to sit where two screens meet, such as a quality company at a value price because of a temporary problem.

## Theme sweep

State the thesis; map the value chain; separate direct from indirect beneficiaries and pure plays from diversified companies; judge who is already priced for it and who is not; look for second-order beneficiaries the market has not yet linked to the theme.

## Judging candidates

- Crowding: how widely held it is, the level of short interest, how many analysts cover it. A crowded idea has less left in it.
- A contrarian idea needs a catalyst. Early without one is the same as wrong.
- A short needs higher conviction than a long, because timing is harder and the loss is not limited.

## Output

- **Sector view**: the six sections above, with the competitor table and the debate.
- **Idea list**: five to ten names, each with direction, a one-sentence thesis, a metric table against peers, why it may be mispriced and what the market is missing, the catalyst, the key risk and the next research step. Include the method and filters used.

## Constraints

- Do not fill a screen from memory with names "that probably qualify"; unverified membership is marked as such.
- Do not present a threshold as an industry standard.
- Dated market facts are labelled with their date.
- This is idea generation, not investment advice.
