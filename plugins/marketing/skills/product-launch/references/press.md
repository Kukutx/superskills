# Press

Read when the launch includes pitching journalists, podcasts or reacting to a news story. A launch with no press work does not need it.

## Before pitching

- Earned coverage is a practice of four to eight weeks, not one send. Relationships come first: follow the journalist's beat, offer something useful with no ask (a data point, a correction), and be the source who returns a clean quotable sentence within minutes.
- **The list is 20 to 40 journalists who really cover this subject**, each checked by reading their last five pieces, not a database of hundreds. Leave out anyone who has not published for half a year, anyone who says they take no pitches, and generic editor addresses. Weight fit to the beat most, then reach and past interaction, then how recently they covered the topic; the best matches get an original pitch, the middle a light adaptation, the rest are skipped.
- **A data story**: turn proprietary data into a benchmark, find the one surprising number, add the method, and give it its own page so an article can be written around that number. A milestone alone is not a story; a milestone attached to a change, a lesson or a small-against-large frame is.
- One core asset (a data report, a point of view, the founding story) is reused as posts, an article, a guest piece and a talk, so a journalist who looks finds a public trail.

## The pitch

- Subject of about 50 characters that states the specific thing and promises the story, not the product. Prefixes such as "Exclusive", "Data" or a reply to the journalist's own article are honest signals when true. "[Company] announces…", "Story idea for you" and anything with "innovative" or "revolutionary" are deleted unread.
- Body: one sentence on why it is for this journalist, the story in one sentence, what it means for their readers, the evidence (data, a customer, a quotation, a link) and a clear ask.
- **Follow-up**: once after about three days and only with new information; once more after about a week with a different angle; then stop until there is something really new. No "bumping this up".
- **What kills a pitch**: a wrong name or outlet; a subject the journalist says they do not cover; an attached PDF instead of the points in the body; a long signature; other journalists in copy; asking for a non-disclosure agreement first; a story that cannot actually be told because no customer will speak and there is no data.
- **Embargo** only with journalists who have kept one before, with date, time and time zone stated; a broken embargo ends the relationship. **An exclusive** goes to a top outlet, with its scope stated honestly and a plan ready for the day after it runs.

## Reacting to the news

Do not: a tragedy, accident or death; a political subject the brand does not already speak on; a field where the company has no real standing; a story more than about two days old; an angle that amounts to "our product solves this".

Score what is left on timeliness, relevance and originality of the view (double weight each), then authority and how long the story will last. A low score is a no, and so is a view that is only disagreement without grounds. Publish the short take on the company's own site first and refer to it in the pitch; with no time to publish, it is not ready.

## Podcasts and expert requests

- Listen to several episodes first, pitch a specific topic rather than a wish to be a guest, and bring proof, a short bio and suggested questions. Say the company name and its category together and say numbers out loud, because the transcript is what gets quoted and indexed. Prepare two or three stories with real figures.
- Answering journalists' requests for expert comment pays only with a few hours every week for about three months; with an hour a week, pitch directly instead. A reply is short: who you are, one line of credentials, a two-sentence answer, one specific quotable example. Early replies are the ones that get used. Selling the product, generic advice and asking for a link get the reply dropped.

## Not worth paying for

Paid press-release distribution at an early stage, "as featured in" badge schemes and paid coverage, and networks selling guest-post links.
