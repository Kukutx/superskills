# Product Positioning

## Use

Use when the unresolved question is **who this is for, what problem/value matters, or why it is meaningfully different**.

Do not use just to polish already-decided store or marketing copy. Once positioning is stable, downstream copy/asset Skills should own execution.

## Workflow

1. Define the product category in plain language.
2. Identify the specific audience and problem worth solving.
3. Separate benefits from features.
4. Identify differentiators that are actually defensible; do not invent proof. Compare against the three to five alternatives the audience would actually choose instead, including doing nothing; state the condition under which each claim holds, say where an alternative is stronger, and date any evidence about competitors.
5. Choose the smallest set of messaging pillars that can stay consistent across product, store and marketing surfaces.
6. Adapt tone and terminology to the requested market/audience.

## Output

Default:

- **Positioning statement**
- **Target audience + core problem**, and who it is not for
- **Primary benefits**
- **Differentiators / proof needed**
- **Messaging pillars**
- **Short headline/slogan options** when useful
- **Claims to avoid**

## Constraints

- Avoid hype words that add no meaning.
- Do not invent traction, reviews, customer proof, performance or scientific/legal claims.
- Do not force Europe/US conventions when the user targets another market.
- Prefer one strong position over many vague audience segments.
- Mark audience and pain-point statements that have no data behind them as assumptions.
- If differentiation is weak, say what evidence/product decision is missing instead of fabricating uniqueness.
- Do not keep rewriting downstream copy once the positioning decision is complete unless the user asks for it.
- Use language customers can understand without internal product jargon.
