# Budget and Measurement

Read for a growth plan that has to justify a budget, set quarterly targets or decide who does the work. A campaign brief with a given budget does not need it.

## Choosing the budget method

- **Share of revenue** suits a business with a known acquisition cost and a stated stance on profit or burn; it answers "is the target reasonable at this stance". The share has to match the ambition: a small share alongside a goal to triple revenue are two signals that contradict each other, and the plan says so.
- **Goal-backward** suits a stated target and answers "how much does it take". Walk through it in four steps when explaining a budget to someone else: revenue to be added ÷ annual revenue per customer = customers needed; × blended acquisition cost = raw cost; ÷ annual retention rate = budget; plus the experiment allowance.

## Acquisition cost

Blended acquisition cost counts everything spent to win customers in the period: marketing people at full cost, media, tools, content production, agency or contractor fees, outbound sales people when outbound is used, and the CRM and analytics behind them, divided by the new customers of the same period. A business that "does not advertise" still has an acquisition cost, hidden in the founder's time, the content team, the SEO contractor and the trade-show stand.

With no history, one year of revenue from the smallest paid plan stands in as a placeholder, labelled as one and replaced by the measured figure after the first period.

## Unit economics table

Revenue per customer per month, blended acquisition cost, annual retention, rough lifetime value (monthly revenue × 12 ÷ annual churn) and their ratio. Any unknown among them is the first open question of the plan. State the current marketing spend as a share of revenue, and which stage of funding the business is in.

Every recommendation is tied to what the current budget can pay for: an action is either executable now or marked as future with the condition that unlocks it. When the user underestimates what an action needs, say so; when there is more money than the funnel can use, do not invent spending.

## Honest forecasts

The annual target and the budget are commitments; a month-by-month forecast is an illustration. Write annual goals, quarterly indicators and kill conditions, and adjust at the quarterly review; a monthly miss is not a failure.

Growth is linear stretches joined by steps, each step caused by something deliberate: a new segment, a major product extension, a new channel. A percentage growth rate falls as the base grows; that is arithmetic. Channels mature differently: search takes months to build and then lasts, paid works fast with diminishing returns, content compounds slowly, partnerships pay off in bursts, outbound is predictable and limited by people, press produces spikes of awareness. Start the next channel before the current one flattens.

## What to measure

The headline metric is not revenue itself, which is an outcome. Choose by model:

| Model | Headline metric |
| --- | --- |
| B2B subscription | net revenue retention |
| consumer subscription app | lifetime value against acquisition cost, blended |
| two-sided marketplace | liquidity and take rate |
| developer tool | weekly active developers and conversion to paid |
| content or media | daily active readers and revenue per session |
| non-subscription store | repeat rate, order value and frequency |

Three to five indicators per quarter, each specific ("day-30 retention from 22% to 30%"), measurable from a source that is already connected, ambitious but reachable, and tied to a decision: what follows if it is missed. An indicator with no data behind it turns "set up the tracking" into a named task.

Quarter by quarter the emphasis usually moves from fixing leaks and foundations, to validating a channel, to scale, to the share of growth that arrives without paid spend.

Every channel and project carries a kill condition written in advance (for a paid channel, for example, an acquisition cost at twice the target after a meaningful spend over 30 days), and the plan names guardrail metrics for brand and unit economics that no experiment may breach.

## Emphasis by business model

- **B2B subscription**: search and content lead the top of the funnel, buyer and user are often different people, expansion is often the largest lever.
- **Consumer subscription app**: the store listing and onboarding are the largest levers, value has to appear within minutes, paywall structure and trial length matter, and the default billing period changes lifetime value more than most campaigns do.
- **Hardware with software**: two activations (unboxing and software sign-up) and the bridge between them.
- **Marketplace**: two funnels and two onboardings; early effort goes to supply, later to demand.
- **Developer tool**: the first successful build or integration is the activation, documentation is the onboarding, and paid acquisition usually comes late.
- **Non-subscription store**: the first purchase is the activation; cart recovery, repeat purchase and order value follow.

## Who does the work

Strategy stays inside: positioning, knowledge of the customer and direction cannot be outsourced. Execution can be: specialist delivery, burst capacity, anything with a clear scope and success measure. A brief to an outside party is specific ("twenty ads across four ad groups by month end, benchmark in the brief"), not "help with marketing". Three functions need an owner even when one person holds them all: demand (growth marketing), story (product marketing) and trust (content). A first marketing hire is a strategist with two deep skills, not a single-channel operator. Do not hire an agency to postpone a strategy discussion, and do not hire full time for a six-month sprint.

## Before delivering a growth plan

- Unconfirmed data is marked and listed as an open question, not guessed.
- Uncomfortable metrics are stated plainly in the current state.
- Work the team has already done is acknowledged; drafted-but-not-launched items each get the reason they are blocked and an owner.
- Open questions are ordered by impact, each saying what it blocks; an unknown or disputed acquisition cost comes first.
- The 90-day roadmap has an owner on every line and runs in four sprints: unblock, build foundations, accelerate, compound.
