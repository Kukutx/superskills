# Marketing Plan

## Use

Use for three jobs: a campaign brief (a push with a start, an end and one goal), a growth plan for the next 90 days to 12 months, and "I don't know how to promote this" where the answer is a short list of things worth trying.

Who the product is for and why it is different belongs to `marketing/product-positioning`; this Skill assumes that is settled. Release readiness, beta stages and launch-day sequencing belong to `marketing/product-launch`. Which topics to publish belongs to `marketing/content-strategy`. Judging a running ad account belongs to `marketing/paid-ads`. The words themselves belong to `writing/marketing-copy`.

## Domain inputs and blockers

- **Always needed**: the objective, the audience and the timeline. A budget is optional; without one, deliver a channel-neutral plan and mark each place where a budget would change the choice.
- **For a growth plan, also**: current spend, revenue per customer, retention, who does the work and how many hours they have, which channels already exist and how they perform, what has been tried, and the stage the product is in.
- **Cost to acquire a customer.** When it is unknown, that is the open question with the largest effect on the plan; list it first among the open questions and do not hide it inside an assumption.
- **Targets come from the user's own history.** With no baseline, a target is written as an assumption to be replaced after the first measured period. Do not supply a number to make the plan look complete.
- If the audience and the difference cannot be stated in one sentence each from what was supplied, say that positioning is the missing piece before planning channels.

## Market check and objective

1. **Market quality gate.** Rate the problem by size multiplied by how often it occurs. Large and frequent is the strongest market; small and rare is the weakest, and the plan says so plainly in its open questions because no channel mix repairs it.
2. **One objective type**, with metrics that match it:

| Type | What is measured |
| --- | --- |
| Awareness | reach, impressions, share of voice |
| Consideration | engagement, email signups, webinar attendance |
| Conversion | signups, demos, purchases, pipeline |
| Retention | lower churn, expansion, NPS |
| Advocacy | referrals, reviews, user-made content |

3. The objective names a number, a segment and a date ("300 trial signups from freelance designers within eight weeks of release"), and the primary KPI is that same number.
4. **Audience in one sentence**: [role] at [kind of company or situation], struggling with [pain], wanting [outcome]; finds solutions through [channels] and cares most about [priority]. A full persona is not needed for planning.
5. **Message order**: why should I care, what is the solution, why you, what do I do next. The "why you" includes the comparison with doing nothing.
6. Message traps to remove: arguing against a competitor instead of a customer need, more than one or two differentiators, category words the customer does not use, features in place of outcomes.

## Choosing channels and actions

- Pick channels by where the audience already is, the buying stage, the budget, the assets that exist and past results. The channel the team is most used to is not a reason.
- Tag every action with its stage: acquisition, activation, retention, referral or revenue. Brand and content cut across the stages and are not stages of their own. A plan whose actions all carry the acquisition tag has no answer for what happens after the click.
- Each action states its mechanism (why it should produce the result) and a leading indicator that moves within weeks. An action that cannot be measured is written as an experiment with a kill condition and a date.
- Build a portfolio that compounds, not a list of events: spread across more than one channel, reuse one asset in several places, and plan the next channel before the current one flattens, since each channel saturates.
- For an ideas shortlist, filter by stage, money and speed, then recommend three to five and stop:

| Filter | Options |
| --- | --- |
| Stage | pre-launch: waitlist with referral, early-bird price, launch-platform preparation. Early: search content, community, founder-led selling. Growth: paid, partnerships, events. Scale: brand, new countries |
| Money | free: content, community, social, review and comment marketing. Low: targeted ads, sponsorships, free tools |
| Speed | fast: ads, email, social. Medium: content, search, community. Slow: brand |

Each recommended idea carries five things: the idea, why it fits this product, the first two or three steps, the expected result, and the resources needed.

## Calendar and budget

- Schedule backward from the milestones, and write the dependencies down (the landing page is live before any ad points at it). Cover each funnel stage, group pieces by theme, and leave about 20% of the slots empty for opportunistic content.
- Production lead times to plan with when the user has none of their own (working defaults): blog post 3–5 working days, email 2–3, social post 1–2, landing page 5–7, video 2–4 weeks, ebook or report 2–4 weeks.
- Every content piece is marked must-have or nice-to-have, so the plan survives a lost week.
- **Budget, two methods.** Goal-backward: (new annual revenue target ÷ annual revenue per customer) × acquisition cost per customer ÷ annual retention rate. Or the user's actual figure. "Marketing as a share of revenue" rules of thumb range from 5% to 40% with no source behind them, so none is used as a target here.
- Acquisition cost in that formula is the blended one: salaries, content and tools as well as media.
- Working defaults for the split, to be replaced by results: about 70% on what already works, 20% on the next channel, 10% on experiments; inside paid spend the most proven channel takes 60–70% and new channels 15–20%. Add 10–20% on top of a goal-backward budget for experiments, hold 10–15% as contingency, count production cost inside the budget, and re-split monthly.
- Risks: two or three, each with its mitigation. The usual three are a slipping timeline, an audience that does not respond, and a channel that underperforms.

## Output

- **Campaign brief**: overview (name, one-line summary, the measurable objective, secondary goals); audience; key messages (one core message, three or four supporting points with their proof); channel plan (reason, format, volume and budget per channel); calendar table (week, piece, channel, owner or note, status); pieces needed with must-have or nice-to-have; metrics (the primary KPI with its target, three to five secondary KPIs, how each is tracked and how often it is reported); budget, only when one was given; risks; next steps.
- **Growth plan**: summary; the market-quality verdict and open questions; current state; actions grouped by stage with mechanism and leading indicator; the 90-day sequence; the 12-month outlook as direction, not dates; budget and split; the measurement plan.
- **Ideas shortlist**: three to five ideas in the five-part form, ordered, with the one to start this week.

## Constraints

- Do not present an unsourced benchmark as an industry fact; every default above is labelled as one and the user's data overrides it.
- Do not fill a missing baseline, conversion rate or acquisition cost with a typical value; the gap is shown and the plan says what the first weeks must measure.
- Do not quietly decide positioning to make the plan work; name the gap.
- Budget figures here are planning arithmetic on the user's inputs, not financial advice.
