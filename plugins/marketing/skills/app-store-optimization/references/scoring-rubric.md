# ASO Scoring Rubric

Read when a listing is being scored. The anchors are a working rubric, not a store formula; the counts in them (ratings volume, languages, update age) are defaults with no official source, so state the evidence behind each score rather than the band alone.

## Title and subtitle (short description on Google Play)

| Score | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| 9–10 | brand plus a high-value term in the title, complementary terms in the subtitle, no word repeated across the two, space almost fully used, purpose clear at a glance |
| 7–8 | good terms, several characters wasted, purpose clear |
| 5–6 | terms present but poorly placed, or repeated across fields |
| 3–4 | title is only a brand name or generic, subtitle weak or missing |
| 1–2 | no keyword thinking, title does not say what the app does |

Check: characters used against the limit, whether the main term is in the title, repetition between fields, filler words, and claims such as "best" or "#1" that stores reject.

## Description

| Score | App Store | Google Play |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9–10 | first three lines carry the value proposition; features, benefits, proof and a call to action in a scannable structure; promotional text in use | first three sentences carry relevant terms; terms spread naturally; formatted and structured, with a call to action |
| 5–6 | opens with "Welcome to…" filler; no proof or call to action | terms very sparse, or repeated until it reads as stuffing |
| 3–4 | one block of text; nothing of value above the fold | the same, with no structure |

Judge the lines above the fold first, and whether features are tied to benefits.

## Visual assets

| Score | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| 9–10 | a full set of captioned screenshots that tell a story in order, one benefit each; a preview video; a distinctive icon |
| 7–8 | a good captioned set with no video, or a video with some unclear screenshots |
| 5–6 | enough screenshots but weak or no captions, a generic icon, raw screens placed side by side |
| 3–4 | three or four uncaptioned screenshots with no story |
| 1–2 | fewer than three, or unprocessed raw UI |

Check: the first three screenshots; captions of five to seven words with one message each; an icon with no text and a strong shape; the feature graphic on Google Play; localized visuals for non-English markets.

## Ratings and reviews

| Score | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| 9–10 | 4.5 or above on a large volume, recent reviews positive, the developer answers criticism, reviews keep arriving |
| 7–8 | 4.0 to 4.4 on a solid volume |
| 5–6 | 3.5 to 3.9, mixed recent reviews, no replies |
| 3–4 | 3.0 to 3.4, few ratings, negative themes appearing |
| 1–2 | below 3.0, very few ratings, no engagement |

Read the five to ten most recent visible reviews for sentiment and recurring complaints (crashes, price, usability), whether and how the developer replies, and the trend.

## Metadata and freshness

| Score | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| 9–10 | updated within a month, well localized for its markets, best-fit category, events or promotional content in use, privacy details complete |
| 7–8 | updated within two months, several languages |
| 5–6 | updated within three months, a few languages |
| 3–4 | three to six months without an update, one or two languages, a doubtful category |
| 1–2 | more than six months stale, one language, a poor category |

Also check the age rating, whether "what's new" text says anything of value, and whether a less crowded category fits.

## Conversion signals

Price transparency, in-app purchase names that convey value, the install band shown on Google Play, developer recognition, editorial badges, awards or press quoted in the description (real ones only), the developer's other apps, and openness about privacy.

## Adjusting for brand maturity

| Dimension | Dominant brand | Established | Challenger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a title that is only the brand name | can score 8 or more, since the brand is the keyword | scored normally | scored low |
| lifestyle screenshots instead of captioned UI | equal credit | benefit of the doubt | scored as missing captions |
| generic "what's new" on a very frequent release rhythm | can score 8 or more | scored normally | scored low |
| description | judged on conversion only | benefit of the doubt on strategy | textbook standard |
| no preview video | acceptable when recognition is near universal | noted | counted |

Before marking down a larger brand, ask whether the choice is an oversight or a decision made with data the reviewer does not have.

## Comparing with competitors

Score every app with the same six dimensions, put them side by side (title terms, rating, screenshots, video, description terms, last update, total), list the terms competitors target in their titles and descriptions that the user's listing does not, and turn the gaps into changes. Treat both stores' differences the same way for every app compared.
