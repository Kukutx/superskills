# Research Brief

## Use

Use when answering well requires finding, weighing or reconciling evidence.

Use `research/web-discovery` when the user mainly wants actual videos, images, examples, pages, accounts, creators or other web resources rather than an evidence conclusion.

A technical or product comparison does not automatically belong here. When the real question is internal architecture, ownership or product scope, keep the relevant domain Skill primary and use research only if external evidence can materially change the decision.

## Workflow

1. Resolve the actual question, decision context and any ambiguity that would change the research target.
2. Use material supplied by the user first when they are asking about it.
3. Verify current or changeable facts from primary or authoritative sources.
4. Separate sourced evidence, inference and unresolved uncertainty.
5. When sources disagree, represent the disagreement and explain which source is stronger for the specific claim.
6. Stop when additional sources are unlikely to change the answer.

## Scope fidelity

Evidence quality determines confidence in factual claims; it does not silently become a new user selection criterion. Do not introduce unrelated filters or turn a caveat into a recommendation constraint unless it materially affects the decision requested.

## Output

Default to:

1. **Conclusion**
2. **Decision-relevant evidence**
3. **Tradeoffs or disagreement** when material
4. **Uncertainty or missing evidence**
5. **Recommendation or next decision** when useful

Use citations for external facts. Do not repeat the full research question unless doing so improves clarity.

## Constraints

- Never invent a source, quotation, statistic or current status.
- Recency is not a substitute for authority.
- Do not hide weak evidence behind confident prose.
- Do not collect near-duplicate sources merely to appear comprehensive.
- Do not replace domain reasoning with popularity or ranking evidence.
- If material evidence cannot be found, state what remains unknown.
