# Psychology Reflection sources

Maintenance-only. These sources support evidence boundaries and practical reflection methods. They do **not** authorize casual diagnosis or make the Skill a substitute for individualized psychotherapy or medical care.

## High-trust anchors

- **National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH), Psychotherapies** — overview of psychotherapy and evidence-based elements including CBT-style work with automatic thoughts, emotions and behavior; also emphasizes individualized treatment and professional care for mental disorders.
  - https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/psychotherapies

- **World Health Organization, Psychological self-help interventions (2026)** — current WHO implementation guidance for structured psychological self-help, featuring Step-by-Step and Doing What Matters in Times of Stress. Useful for the boundary between practical self-help and formal care.
  - https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240120785

- **World Health Organization, Doing What Matters in Times of Stress** — practical stress-management material using skills such as grounding, noticing/naming, unhooking from difficult thoughts, making room and values-oriented action.
  - https://www.who.int/publications/b/53604

- **Association for Contextual Behavioral Science, ACT psychological flexibility model** — primary professional source for ACT processes such as acceptance, defusion, present-moment awareness, values and committed action. Use as a framework source, not as a reason to force ACT terminology into ordinary answers.
  - https://contextualscience.org/six_core_processes_act

- **APA Dictionary of Psychology, attachment style** — concise professional definition and historical/theoretical context for attachment-style language. Useful as a terminology anchor; attachment categories should not be treated as casual chat diagnoses or fixed identities.
  - https://dictionary.apa.org/attachment-style

## Source-use rules

Prefer sources in roughly this order when a claim needs verification:

```text
current public-health / clinical authority
-> professional psychological organization
-> peer-reviewed systematic review / guideline
-> high-quality primary research for a narrow question
-> reputable educational secondary source
```

For diagnosis, treatment, medication, suicidality, severe impairment or another high-stakes clinical question, verify current authoritative guidance at execution time. Do not rely only on this maintenance file.

## Framework discipline

CBT, ACT, behavioral analysis, attachment theory and related models can supply useful hypotheses or exercises. They are not interchangeable proof that a user's hidden cause has been identified.

Keep the runtime distinction:

```text
observed pattern
-> tentative mechanism
-> proportionate experiment
-> observed result
```

Do not promote popular but weakly supported mind-reading claims such as deterministic body-language lie detection, microexpression certainty or “dark psychology” manipulation techniques as established psychological science.

## Expansion rule

Do not add a permanent reference for every therapy school, personality model or self-help trend. Add source depth only when repeated real use changes decisions or exposes a stable evidence boundary the concise Skill cannot safely express.
